Read-to-Unlock Quest

A quest where you have to read the project disclosure before you can submit a review, and where farmers who share answers get caught automatically.

How it works

Most quests pay for cheap tasks like follows and retweets, and can't check the task that matters. This one pays for reading, and protects the person who checks the submissions.

The case: an asset-information platform invites people from a waitlist to read a project disclosure and submit a useful question or review.

Three steps

Step 1Easy tasks

Follow, join, share. Checked automatically. 5 XP each, capped at 15, because they're easy to fake.

Step 2Read and pass the quiz

Open the disclosure, answer 5 questions. Need 4 right, one attempt. Passing unlocks step 3.

Step 3One submission

One question or review per person. A human reads it and scores it 1 to 5. 3 or above is approved.

Optional: sign a testnet acknowledgement for 10 XP. Wallet is never required. Someone with no wallet can reach the top.

Why the quiz alone isn't enough

Anyone can paste the disclosure into ChatGPT and pass the quiz. So the quiz is only there to keep lazy people out of the review queue. It doesn't stop farmers.

What stops farmers: everyone gets one of five versions of the disclosure. The versions are the same except for a few numbers. If someone's submission quotes a number from a version they were never shown, they copied it from someone else. That's caught automatically, without anyone judging the writing.

The main finding

Turn off the version check and the approval rate goes up, while 2 farmers get paid. The farmers wrote good submissions, so the reviewer approved them. Approval rate can't tell you if you're being farmed. You need a check that doesn't depend on how good the writing is.

What counts as on-chain

Only the optional testnet acknowledgement that someone read the disclosure. Everything that needs judgment, like the review score, stays off-chain. Which version someone got also stays private, because publishing it would give farmers the answers.

Stop rules

  • Version mismatch rate above 10% in a week: pause and swap in new versions, because an answer key is going around.

Synthetic dataset

All 12 people are made up. Each one tests a specific case.

PRD

Problem

Quest campaigns pay for cheap tasks that are easy to fake, and the one task that matters, a real review, needs a person to read it. That person has limited hours. And a farmer using AI writes reviews good enough to get approved.

Goal

More approved submissions from people who actually read the disclosure, with no farmers paid, and a review queue one person can keep up with.

Users

  • Participants, with or without a wallet
  • The reviewer, who has about 3 to 4 hours a week
  • The growth lead, who decides how many people to invite

Backlog, in priority order

1. Duplicate check and version assignment. Nothing else is trustworthy without it.

  • Same device, email or KYC hash as an existing account: earns 0 XP, can't submit.
  • Each person gets a random version at sign-up. It's stored on the server and never shown in the page, URL or on-chain.

2. Quiz gate with one attempt.

  • Submission is blocked on the server until the quiz is passed, not just hidden.
  • One attempt. A fail starts a 24 hour wait and the retry uses different questions.
  • The locked screen says what unlocks it.

3. Automatic checks before review.

  • A number quoted from a different version: rejected, with a reason and a way to appeal.
  • A submission that quotes no numbers is never penalised.
  • Submitted in under 30 seconds: held for a person to look at, not banned.

4. Review queue.

  • Oldest first, one at a time, minutes spent recorded.
  • Scoring guide with an example of a 2 and a 4 so reviewers agree.
  • Dashboard shows queue size, approval rate and mismatch rate.

5. XP, levels and optional acknowledgement.

  • No level needs a wallet. A wallet decliner can top the table.
  • A failed signature only loses its own 10 XP and can be retried.
  • Easy-task XP capped at 15.

Channel experiment

Channel: Telegram announcement, two links split at random. Not run.

  • A, reward-led: "Complete quests and climb the leaderboard."
  • B, reading-led: "Read the disclosure, pass the quiz, and the team reads your review."

Expected result: B gets fewer sign-ups but more approved submissions per sign-up and a smaller review queue.

Decision rule: pick B if its main metric is at least 5 points higher and reviewer minutes per approval are no worse. Don't decide on approval rate alone.

Size: 200 eligible people per link, up to 2 weeks. This is an assumption, not a calculation. It's also more than one reviewer can handle, so the test needs a second reviewer or fewer people.

Weekly decision memo, week 1

Numbers

  • 12 signed up, 11 unique, 8 submitted, 5 reached the reviewer, 4 approved
  • Approval rate 80%, version mismatch rate 25%
  • 2 of 4 approved people never connected a wallet

What happened

U09 and U10 both quoted a number from version A, but they were given versions B and D. They shared answers. Both wrote reviews good enough for a 4, so without the version check they'd have been approved and paid. With the check off, approval rate actually rises from 80% to 86%. That's why approval rate alone can't be trusted.

Decisions

  • Mismatch rate is 25%, well above the 10% line. Swap in new versions before the next invite batch.
  • One reviewer at 40 reviews a week can handle about 190 sign-ups a week. Cap invites there until a second reviewer is added.

Abuse risks

  • Someone running 5 accounts can read all 5 versions. This stops mass farming, not a determined small farmer.
  • Multiple devices and emails can beat the duplicate check.
  • Two reviewers may score the same review differently.
  • A typo in one version could reject honest people.

Next validation step

Take 20 real submissions from a past campaign, with permission, and have two people score them separately. That checks whether the scoring guide works and how long a review really takes. Every capacity number assumes 5 minutes.

Next for engineering

Build backlog items 1 and 2: duplicate check, version assignment and the quiz gate, all enforced on the server.

Next for ops

Write the 5 versions and have a second person check they only differ in the intended numbers. Write the scoring guide. Clear held accounts daily.
